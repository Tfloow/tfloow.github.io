Through SANDA, we propose a self-adaptive, variable-length, bit-serial AI accelerator which, to the best of our knowledge, is the first to support **on-the-fly mantissa length adaptation** for LLM inference. This allows for a significant throughput increase while retaining most of the LLM's quality. Additionally, SANDA supports **on-the-fly KV cache compression**, which is crucial for long-context inference.

This work is an improvement over the state-of-the-art [ANDA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15982) accelerator, which requires expensive offline calibration to determine optimal mantissa lengths.

## Abstract

**LLMs** face major memory and computational constraints as models grow in size. The **BFP number format** addresses this by **sharing exponents across groups of values** to reduce memory traffic. Recent work proposes reducing the mantissa length within BFP to further lower both memory traffic and computational cost. 

However, most variable-length BFP solutions require **expensive offline calibration** to determine optimal mantissa lengths, preventing runtime adaptation at the edge. Moreover, existing accelerators **lack support for KV cache compression**, which creates a significant memory bottleneck for long-context inference. 

We propose **SANDA, a self-adaptive, variable-length, bit-serial AI accelerator**. At the software level, we design an online algorithm that tunes the mantissa length on-the-fly using a lightweight proxy for perplexity. At the hardware level, we design an algorithm-aware bit-serial accelerator that efficiently computes BFP matrix multiplication operations and compresses the KV cache on-the-fly. 

SANDA achieves comparable or lower perplexity degradation than ANDA with a **throughput increase of 2.37x while reducing the number of calibration iterations by over 60%. Combined with KV cache quantization**, our method maintains **sub-1% perplexity degradation** while reducing the memory footprint of the **KV cache by a factor of 2.46x** compared to standard FP16, saving approximately 10 GB at 30,000-token context length for Llama 2 7B.
